Flights to Orlando

Round-trip flights to Orlando average ~$299 per person from most major US cities. Orlando International (MCO) is one of the most heavily served leisure airports in the country — Spirit, Frontier, Southwest, and all major carriers compete hard on this route, keeping fares reasonable year-round.

MCO vs. Sanford (SFB)

Orlando International (MCO) is the primary airport, 25 minutes from Disney and 20 minutes from Universal. Rideshare to I-Drive or Kissimmee runs $25–$35. It’s the right choice for most travelers.

Orlando Sanford (SFB) is a smaller airport about 45 minutes north of Disney, served mainly by Allegiant. Fares can be $40–$80 cheaper round-trip from select cities (Midwest, Southeast), but rideshare to Disney runs $60–$80 and the airport is inconvenient. Only worth it if Allegiant serves your home city at a significant discount.

When to fly

Book 3–6 weeks out. Orlando is a year-round leisure destination so prices rarely crater, but early September after Labor Day and the two weeks after Thanksgiving are the cheapest windows. Avoid Christmas week, spring break (mid-March through April), and summer (June–August) — both park crowds and airfare spike significantly.

Hotels in Orlando: Stay Off-Site

This is the single biggest money decision for an Orlando trip. Disney and Universal on-property hotels run $250–$450/night. Off-site hotels on International Drive or in Kissimmee average $80–$130/night for comparable quality — a difference of $1,000–$2,000 per week.

International Drive (I-Drive)

I-Drive is the best off-site base. Hotels here run $85–$120/night, the I-Ride Trolley connects the strip for $2 a ride, and you’re 15–20 minutes from both Disney and Universal by rideshare ($18–$25 each way). Dozens of restaurant options at every price point are within walking distance, which matters when you’re budgeting food.

Kissimmee

Kissimmee is even cheaper at $65–$100/night, especially along US-192. It’s closest to Disney (10–15 min) but further from Universal and I-Drive. Great option if Disney is your primary focus and you have a car or are comfortable with rideshares.

What you give up staying off-site

On-property hotels include free Disney resort transportation (buses, monorail, boats) and 30-minute early park entry. Those perks are real — but at $300+/night extra per week, you’re paying roughly $2,100 for transportation and 30 extra minutes per day. It doesn’t pencil out on a $2,000 total budget.

Getting Around: The Rental Car Debate

Orlando is the most car-dependent city on this list, but whether you need a rental depends entirely on your itinerary.

Skip the rental car if…

You’re staying on I-Drive and only visiting Disney and Universal. Rideshare from I-Drive to either park runs $18–$25 each way. For a 7-day trip with 2 park days and a couple of I-Drive dinners, you’ll spend roughly $80–$100 on Uber/Lyft total. A 7-day rental at $50/day is $350 — plus $30/day Disney parking adds another $60 for 2 park days. Rideshare wins by ~$330.

Rent a car if…

You want to visit Kennedy Space Center (45 min east on the 528), do an Everglades day trip (3.5 hrs south), hit Cocoa Beach (1 hr), or visit multiple parks across the week. In that case, renting for 2–3 days specifically for those excursions (~$100–$150 total) makes sense. Avoid renting for the full week just for park days — Disney parking alone kills the savings.

Theme Parks: The Budget Reality

Theme park tickets are Orlando’s biggest budget wildcard. A single-day Disney ticket starts at ~$109 and can reach $189 depending on date. Universal is $109–$149/day. On a $2,000 total budget, 2 park days is realistic; 4–5 days requires cutting significantly elsewhere.

The smart play: Do 1 Disney day (Magic Kingdom or EPCOT — pick one) and 1 Universal day. Spend the remaining days at Kennedy Space Center, Disney Springs, Universal CityWalk, and Cocoa Beach — all free or low-cost. That itinerary hits the highlights without blowing the budget on park tickets.

Skip Genie+ on a budget trip. Disney’s Lightning Lane / Genie+ add-on costs $25–$35/day per person on top of admission. On a $2,000 budget, go early (rope drop), hit the biggest rides first, and accept the queues — it’s not worth the extra $70+ for 2 days.

Food & Drinks Budget

Food is where Orlando budgets quietly collapse — almost entirely because of park dining. A quick-service meal inside Disney runs $15–$25 per person. A table-service dinner at EPCOT can hit $60–$80. The fix is simple: eat breakfast before you enter and dinner after you leave.

Hotel breakfast: Most I-Drive and Kissimmee hotels have included continental breakfast or a grab-and-go option. Even a basic grocery run ($20 at the Publix on I-Drive) covers 7 hotel breakfasts for under $3/day.

Inside the parks: Budget $15–$20 for one quick-service lunch. Skip the character dining and table-service restaurants — they eat a full day’s food budget in one meal.

Dinner on I-Drive: International Drive has everything from $12 Cuban sandwiches to $18 all-you-can-eat Brazilian churrasco. Avoid the tourist-trap restaurants right at the main I-Drive intersections — walk a block off the main strip and prices drop 20–30%.

Drinks: Alcohol inside Disney and Universal is expensive — $12–$16 for a beer, $16–$20 for a cocktail. If you drink, have a beer at the hotel before heading in and limit park drinks to one or two. I-Drive happy hours run until 7 PM at most bars with $5–$7 cocktails.

Tips for Stretching Your $2,000 Orlando Budget

  • Stay off Disney/Universal property — I-Drive hotels at $85–$120/night save $1,000+ vs. on-site over a week
  • Limit yourself to 2 paid park days — Disney Springs and Universal CityWalk are free and genuinely worth a half-day each
  • Check Allegiant at Sanford (SFB) if they serve your home city — can save $50–$80 round-trip
  • Buy Disney tickets at least 7 days in advance online — last-minute tickets at the gate cost more and date-based pricing means mid-week is cheaper than weekends
  • Skip Genie+ — rope drop (arrive 30 min before park open) gets you on the top 2–3 rides before lines form
  • Eat breakfast at the hotel, one quick-service lunch in the park, dinner back on I-Drive
  • Kennedy Space Center is one of the best value days in Florida at $67 — plan it for a non-park day
