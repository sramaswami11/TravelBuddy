Flights to New York

Round-trip flights to New York average ~$299 per person from most major US cities. New York is served by three airports — JFK, LaGuardia (LGA), and Newark (EWR) — and the competition keeps prices reasonable on most routes.

Which airport to fly into

JFK has the most international routes and often the best prices from the East Coast and South. AirTrain + subway to Midtown costs ~$10 and takes about an hour.

LGA is closest to Midtown for domestic flights from Chicago, DC, and Boston, but has fewer budget carrier options. Taxi or rideshare runs $30–$45.

EWR (Newark) frequently has the lowest fares, especially from the Midwest and West Coast. NJ Transit train to Penn Station is ~$18 and takes 30–40 minutes.

What affects the price

Book 4–6 weeks out. New York flights on popular corridors (Chicago, LA, Miami) are competitive but spike sharply for holiday weekends and major events. Avoid Thanksgiving week, New Year’s Eve, and the NYC Marathon weekend.

Hotels in New York

Hotel prices in New York are the highest of any major US domestic destination. The citywide average is ~$200/night, and Midtown Manhattan properties push $250–$400+. Hitting $130/night means being smart about location — it’s achievable, but you need to look in the right places.

Where to stay by budget

Budget ($100–$140/night): Hell’s Kitchen (west side of Midtown), Midtown East (around 30th–40th St), or Long Island City (Queens) — one subway stop from Midtown Manhattan. Rates run 30–40% cheaper than Times Square-area hotels with minimal trade-off.

Mid-range ($150–$200/night): Chelsea, the Financial District, or lower Midtown. Good access to subway lines, more breathing room on price.

Tip: Many NYC hotels charge a “destination fee” or “facilities fee” of $20–$35/night on top of the room rate — functionally the same as a resort fee. Always check the total before booking. A $115 rate with a $30 destination fee is $145/night, or $1,015 for 7 nights.

Getting Around: Skip the Car

Do not rent a car for a New York City trip. The subway is one of the best transit systems in the world, parking runs $40–$60/day at Manhattan garages, and driving in Midtown is a genuine liability. A rental car would add ~$450–$550 to your budget and make the trip worse.

A 7-day unlimited MetroCard runs $34 and covers all subway and local bus rides. Add $15–$20 for occasional rideshares or the AirTrain from JFK and you’re done. That’s the $50 transit line in the breakdown above.

The one exception: If you want to do a day trip to the Catskills, Finger Lakes, or the Jersey Shore, renting a car for a single day (~$60–$80) makes sense. But that’s a day-trip add-on, not a week-long rental.

Food & Drinks Budget

New York has an enormous range of food costs — from $1 pizza slices and $2 bagels to $40 dinner entrees. A realistic daily budget for a solo traveler is ~$70/day, which covers three meals without eating like a tourist every night.

Save money: Dollar pizza is real and excellent — it’s a legitimate lunch strategy. Chinatown and the East Village have full sit-down meals for $12–$18. Midtown delis and bodegas are cheap for breakfast. Save your food budget for one or two dinners at a neighborhood restaurant where locals actually eat.

Watch the drinks bill. Cocktails at Manhattan bars run $18–$24 each. A few drinks out can absorb your entire daily food budget in one sitting. Happy hours (4–7 PM) cut prices significantly at most bars outside the tourist corridors.

Avoid: Times Square restaurants, any place with a host waving you in from the sidewalk, and tourist-targeted spots near major landmarks. Walk two blocks off the main drag and prices drop 30–40%.

Tips for Stretching Your $2,000 NYC Budget

  • Stay in Hell’s Kitchen or Long Island City — 30–40% cheaper than Times Square hotels with subway access to everything
  • Always check the total with destination fees before booking — a $115 rate can be $145+ all-in
  • Do not rent a car — a $34 weekly MetroCard gets you everywhere
  • Use the TKTS booth in Times Square for day-of Broadway tickets at 20–50% off
  • The Staten Island Ferry is free and gives you the best view of the Statue of Liberty — skip the paid ferry
  • Eat one meal a day in Chinatown or a neighborhood spot — $12 vs. $35 for the same quality
