Flights to Miami

Round-trip flights to Miami (MIA) average ~$299 per person from most major US cities. Miami is a major hub with strong competition on key routes, though it doesn’t have quite the rock-bottom pricing of Las Vegas.

What affects the price

Origin city matters. From New York or Boston, flights are often $180–$280 round-trip. From Chicago or Atlanta, $250–$350 is typical. From the West Coast, budget $350–$450.

Avoid holiday travel windows. Miami prices spike sharply around Spring Break (March), Memorial Day, and New Year’s Eve. January and February are peak season for weather-related demand — book early or pay a premium.

Book 4–8 weeks out for the best fares. Last-minute Miami flights on popular routes can be 2× the normal price.

Hotels in Miami

Miami’s average hotel rate is ~$140/night — the highest of any major US domestic destination. South Beach properties in particular push $180–$300+ per night in season. Hitting $100/night means being strategic about location.

Where to stay by budget

Budget ($75–$110/night): Brickell, Midtown, or the northern end of Miami Beach (around 71st–79th St). You’re a 15–20 minute drive or Uber from South Beach, and rates are 30–40% cheaper.

Mid-range ($130–$180/night): South Beach mid-block properties or Coconut Grove. Walking distance to the action, but the bill climbs fast over 7 nights.

Tip: Miami Beach hotels typically add resort fees of $25–$45/night on top of the room rate. Always check the total before booking — a $99 room with a $40 resort fee is $139/night.

Car Rental in Miami

A rental car runs ~$55/day, or ~$385 for 7 days. Whether it’s worth it depends entirely on what you plan to do.

When a car is worth it: If you want to visit the Everglades (45 minutes away), do a day trip to Key West (~3.5 hours down US-1), or explore beyond the South Beach corridor, a car is essential and pays for itself versus tour prices.

When to skip it: If you’re staying in South Beach and your itinerary is beach, restaurants, and nightlife, you don’t need it. Uber and Lyft are reliable, and parking in South Beach runs $20–$40/day at hotels. Skipping the car saves $385 and gives you more food and activity budget.

Food & Drinks Budget

Miami has a wide food range, but South Beach restaurants skew expensive. A realistic daily budget for a solo traveler is ~$60/day — achievable if you eat strategically rather than on Ocean Drive every night.

Save money: Little Havana is one of the best-value dining areas in any major US city. Cuban lunch specials run $10–$14 for a full plate. Café Versailles is a landmark worth the trip. Grab breakfast at a local bakery ($5–$8) and save your food budget for dinner.

Watch the drinks bill. Cocktails on South Beach run $16–$22 each. Happy hours (typically 4–7 PM) cut prices by 30–50% at most bars. One or two happy hour sessions instead of peak-hour drinks saves $40–$60 over the week.

Tips for Stretching Your $2,000 Miami Budget

  • Stay in Brickell or Midtown — 30–40% cheaper than South Beach with easy Uber access
  • Always check the total with resort fees before booking — a $99 rate can be $140+ all-in
  • Skip the car if you’re staying on South Beach — save $385 and avoid $30/day parking
  • Rent a car only if you plan to do the Everglades or Keys — both are worth the drive
  • Eat in Little Havana for lunch — $12 Cuban plates beat $28 tourist-strip sandwiches
  • South Beach is free — your best days cost nothing but sunscreen
