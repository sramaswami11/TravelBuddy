Flights to Los Angeles

Round-trip flights to Los Angeles average ~$299 per person from most major US cities. LAX is the second-busiest airport in the country, served by every major carrier plus Spirit, Frontier, and Southwest, so competition keeps fares competitive year-round.

LAX vs. the alternative airports

LAX (Los Angeles International) is where you’ll almost always find the cheapest fares and the most direct routes. It’s also the most chaotic airport in the US — plan 90 minutes for rideshare/shuttle pickup, not 20. The rental car center is a free shuttle ride from the terminals.

BUR (Hollywood Burbank) is small, fast, and dramatically less stressful. It’s the right choice if you’re spending most of your time in Hollywood, Universal Studios, or the Valley, and fares are sometimes within $20–$40 of LAX. Worth checking before you book.

LGB (Long Beach) is served mainly by Southwest and JetBlue. If you’re flying in from a Southwest hub, check it — it’s 25 miles south of downtown but rideshare is cheaper and the airport is easy. SNA (Orange County) is similar: convenient if Anaheim or Disneyland is in your plans, but adds driving time for anything north of downtown.

When to fly

LA has no true off-season — it’s a year-round destination with stable weather and crowds. The cheapest windows are January–mid-February (after New Year’s, before Valentine’s), and September after Labor Day. Avoid spring break (mid-March through April), Thanksgiving week, and the summer peak (June–August) when both hotel prices and demand spike significantly.

Hotels: The Neighborhood Decision

LA hotel prices vary more by neighborhood than almost any other US city. Santa Monica runs $200–$350/night. Beverly Hills and West Hollywood run $250–$450/night. You can get the same quality of sleep in Koreatown for $95–$130/night — a difference of $700–$2,000 for the week.

Koreatown ($95–$130/night)

Koreatown is the best budget base in LA. It’s central — 15 minutes to Downtown, 20 to Hollywood, 30 to Santa Monica by car. Hotels here are clean, safe, and significantly cheaper than any comparable neighborhood. The bonus: Koreatown has some of the best late-night dining in the city (Korean BBQ, ramen, karaoke) all within walking distance of your hotel, which saves money on dinner.

Inglewood ($90–$115/night)

Inglewood is the cheapest decent option near LAX and makes sense if your flight lands late or departs early. It’s close to SoFi Stadium if you’re catching a game or concert. The tradeoff is distance from most tourist sights — plan 25–35 minutes to Hollywood and 15 minutes to the beach.

Mid-Wilshire / Miracle Mile ($110–$145/night)

A middle ground — close to the LA County Museum of Art (LACMA), the La Brea Tar Pits, and Fairfax Avenue. Slightly pricier than Koreatown but walkable to a few sights without getting in the car, which is rare in LA.

What you give up staying off the Westside

Santa Monica and Venice are genuinely nicer — walkable, beachy, and pleasant in a way that the inland neighborhoods aren’t. But at $200+/night extra, you’re paying roughly $1,400 more for the week to be 20 minutes closer to the ocean you’re going to drive to anyway. It doesn’t pencil out on a $2,000 total budget.

Car Rental: You Need One

Unlike New York or Chicago, a car is not optional in Los Angeles. The city spans 500 square miles, public transit is limited outside of Downtown, and the attractions you actually want to see — Griffith Observatory, Venice Beach, the Getty, Malibu, Universal Studios — are spread across a massive metro area.

What to pay

Compact rentals at LAX average $35–$55/day in 2026. Book 2–3 weeks out for the best rates. Avoid the airport rental counters — the LAX rental car center charges a ~25% airport concession fee. Book a location in Koreatown or Mid-City (Budget, Enterprise, and Hertz all have off-airport locations), take rideshare from the airport to your hotel, then pick up the car the next morning. This saves $70–$120 on a 7-day rental.

Parking costs

Beach parking is mostly free or $3–$8/day at municipal lots (Venice, Santa Monica, Malibu). Griffith Observatory parking is free on the lower lots. Avoid Downtown LA by car if you can — parking runs $20–$40/day in garages. Universal Studios charges $30 for self-parking; skip it and rideshare to Universal for $15–$20 from Hollywood.

Traffic reality

LA traffic is not a myth. The 405 and the 10 are genuinely brutal on weekday mornings and late afternoons. Plan your driving for before 9 AM or after 7 PM. Beach days work well on weekdays; leave Hollywood and Griffith for mornings. Google Maps live traffic in LA is essential — always check before you leave.

Getty Center tip: The museum is free but parking is $20. Take the Big Blue Bus Line 14 from downtown Santa Monica for $1.25, or rideshare for $12–$15 — both are cheaper than parking.

Food & Drinks Budget

Los Angeles has some of the best food in the country at every price point — but it also has some of the most aggressively overpriced tourist restaurants. The key is knowing where not to eat.

Where to eat well and cheaply

Grand Central Market (Downtown) is the best single food stop in LA. Vendors sell everything from $12 pupusas to $14 Thai, all excellent. Go for lunch on a weekday — it gets crowded on weekends but the quality is consistent.

Koreatown is legitimately one of the best food neighborhoods in the country. Korean BBQ runs $25–$40/person for a full table spread. Late-night ramen and tofu soup around $12–$15. If you’re staying here, your dinner budget is in great shape.

Food trucks are everywhere in LA and consistently good. The Kogi BBQ truck, Mariscos Jalisco in East LA, and the Boyle Heights taco trucks are worth seeking out at $3–$6 per item.

In-N-Out Burger — a Double-Double combo is $9. It’s not ironic: it’s just very good fast food. Hit it once.

Where to avoid

Hollywood Boulevard restaurants are tourist traps — $22 burgers, $16 cocktails, mediocre food. The Walk of Fame is worth seeing; eating there is not. Walk two blocks north to Franklin Ave or east to Los Feliz for real neighborhood restaurants.

Drinks: LA bar prices are high — $14–$18 cocktails at Westside bars. Koreatown soju bars and beer+soju combos run $6–$10. Happy hour (3–7 PM) at most bars drops cocktails to $9–$12. If you’re watching the budget, drink at the hotel before going out.

Tips for Stretching Your $2,000 LA Budget

  • Stay in Koreatown or Inglewood — $95–$130/night vs. $200+ on the Westside saves $400–$700 for the week
  • Book your rental car at an off-airport location in Mid-City — avoids the LAX ~25% airport concession surcharge
  • The Getty Center is free and genuinely world-class — take the bus from Santa Monica to skip the $20 parking fee
  • Griffith Observatory at night for the city view is one of the best free experiences in the US — go on a clear weeknight to avoid crowds
  • Rideshare to Universal Studios instead of driving — saves $30 on parking and $15–$20 in gas vs. $12–$20 for the ride
  • Check BUR (Burbank) flights before booking LAX — sometimes within $30 of LAX and dramatically easier to navigate
  • Eat at Grand Central Market for lunch and in Koreatown for dinner — two of the best-value meals in the city
  • Drive PCH (Pacific Coast Highway) north to Malibu on a weekday morning — free, scenic, and genuinely one of the best drives in the country
