Flights to Las Vegas

Round-trip flights to Las Vegas (LAS) average ~$299 per person from most major US cities. Las Vegas is one of the most competitive flight routes in the country — airlines know it drives leisure travel and price accordingly.

What affects the price

Origin city matters most. From Los Angeles or Phoenix, you can find flights under $150 round-trip. From New York or Boston, expect $300–$400. From Atlanta or Chicago, $250–$350 is typical.

Book 3–6 weeks out for the best prices. Last-minute Vegas flights spike significantly on weekends and around major events (CES, Formula 1, New Year’s Eve).

Avoid Friday arrivals and Sunday departures — peak leisure travel days. Tuesday or Wednesday flights are consistently cheaper.

Hotels in Las Vegas

Hotel prices in Las Vegas are unusually volatile. The Strip properties average ~$95/night on weeknights but can jump to $250–$400+ on weekends or during major events. A 7-night trip that spans two weekends will cost significantly more than a mid-week stay.

Where to stay by budget

Budget ($60–$90/night): Off-Strip properties like Circus Circus, Excalibur, or Luxor. Perfectly comfortable, with easy tram or rideshare access to everything.

Mid-range ($90–$150/night): Park MGM, New York-New York, or The LINQ. Good Strip location without the premium.

Tip: Most Vegas hotels charge a daily resort fee ($35–$50) on top of the room rate. Factor this in when comparing prices — it adds ~$245–$350 to a 7-night stay.

Car Rental in Las Vegas

A rental car runs ~$45/day, or ~$315 for 7 days. Las Vegas is one of the few US cities where a car is genuinely optional — the Strip is walkable, Uber/Lyft are cheap, and the monorail connects major properties.

When a car is worth it: If you plan to visit Red Rock Canyon, Hoover Dam, or Valley of Fire — all within 45 minutes of the Strip — a rental car makes day trips much easier and cheaper than tours.

When to skip it: If you’re staying on the Strip and not planning day trips, rideshare will be cheaper than parking fees (~$15–$25/day at most hotels).

Food & Drinks Budget

Food in Las Vegas ranges from cheap to extravagant. A realistic daily food budget for a solo traveler is ~$60/day, covering breakfast, lunch, and a sit-down dinner.

Save money: Grab breakfast at a casino buffet ($15–$20), hit the food court for lunch ($10–$15), and splurge on one nice dinner. Many high-end restaurants on the Strip have excellent lunch menus at half the dinner price.

Watch the drinks bill. Cocktails on the Strip run $15–$20 each. If you’re gambling (even small amounts), drinks at the table or slot machines are typically complimentary.

Tips for Stretching Your $2,000 Budget

  • Travel mid-week (arrive Tuesday, depart Monday) — hotel rates drop 30–50% vs. weekends
  • Book flights 3–6 weeks in advance and avoid major event weekends
  • Compare hotel rates on Booking.com — off-Strip properties are 40% cheaper with easy access
  • Skip the car if you’re not doing day trips — save $315 and use rideshare
  • One Sphere concert or big show is worth budgeting for — it’s uniquely Vegas
  • Red Rock Canyon costs $15 to self-drive — far cheaper than a $90 tour and just as good
