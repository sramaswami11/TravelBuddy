Flights to Chicago

Round-trip flights to Chicago average ~$299 per person from most major US cities. Chicago is served by two airports — O’Hare (ORD) and Midway (MDW) — and the competition between them keeps fares reasonable on most domestic routes.

O'Hare (ORD) vs. Midway (MDW)

O’Hare (ORD) is the main hub for United and American, with the most routes and all international flights. The Blue Line L train runs directly from O’Hare to downtown for $5 and takes about 45 minutes. It’s reliable and runs 24 hours.

Midway (MDW) is Southwest’s Chicago hub and consistently undercuts O’Hare on price, especially from Southern and Midwestern cities. The Orange Line L runs downtown in about 30 minutes for $5. If Southwest serves your route, check Midway first — it can save $40–$80 round-trip.

What affects the price

Book 3–5 weeks out. Chicago is a major hub so flights are rarely outrageously expensive, but prices spike around holidays, the Chicago Marathon (October), and Lollapalooza weekend (August). Shoulder seasons — late April through May and September through October — offer the best mix of price and weather.

Hotels in Chicago

Chicago hotel prices are moderate by major US city standards. The Loop and River North average ~$150–$200/night at mid-range properties, but budget hotels in the same areas reliably come in at ~$110–$140/night if you book a few weeks out.

Where to stay by budget

Budget ($100–$140/night): South Loop and the northern edges of the Loop offer the best value for central locations. River North has options in this range too, though they book up fast on weekends. These put you walking distance from Millennium Park, the Riverwalk, and the L.

Mid-range ($150–$190/night): River North proper, Streeterville, and the Mag Mile corridor. Well-located with more amenities, though weekend rates in River North can surge significantly — always check Friday vs. Sunday arrival.

Tip: Chicago hotels frequently add parking fees of $45–$65/night if you have a car. If you’re driving in, factor that into your hotel budget or look for a property with free parking in a neighborhood slightly further out (Wicker Park, Logan Square) where street parking is easier.

Getting Around: Ride the L

Chicago’s L train (elevated rail) is one of the best transit systems in the US for tourists. Eight color-coded lines connect O’Hare, Midway, the Loop, Navy Pier (via bus), Wrigley Field, and most major neighborhoods. A 7-day unlimited Ventra pass costs $28 and covers all L and CTA bus rides.

Add $10–$15 for occasional rideshares (late nights, Navy Pier, etc.) and you’re comfortably at $40 for the week. That’s the transit line in the breakdown above.

Skip the rental car for a city-only trip. Parking in the Loop runs $35–$55/day, street parking requires knowing the neighborhood, and the L gets you everywhere you actually want to go. A rental adds ~$350–$450 to your budget and mostly creates friction.

The exception: If you want to do a day trip to Indiana Dunes National Park (~1 hr southeast) or the North Shore suburbs, renting a car for a single day (~$55–$75) makes sense. The South Shore Line train also reaches Indiana Dunes for ~$10 round-trip if you prefer transit.

Food & Drinks Budget

Chicago has a reputation as a serious food city, and it earns it at every price point. A solo traveler can eat extremely well on ~$60/day by mixing neighborhood spots with the iconic cheap eats Chicago is famous for.

The Chicago classics are cheap. An authentic Chicago-style hot dog (Vienna Beef, sport peppers, neon relish, no ketchup) runs $4–$6 at spots like Portillo’s or Gene & Jude’s. An Italian beef sandwich at Al’s Beef or Portillo’s is $9–$12. These aren’t budget compromises — they’re what locals actually eat.

Deep-dish pizza: Budget $20–$30 for a sit-down deep-dish meal at Lou Malnati’s, Giordano’s, or Pequod’s. Plan one deep-dish dinner — it’s a full experience, not a quick slice. For everyday pizza, Chicago also has excellent thin-crust (tavern-style) which is faster and cheaper.

Neighborhoods to eat in: Pilsen (Mexican), Chinatown (10 min on the Red Line), Logan Square, and Wicker Park all have full meals for $12–$18. The Mag Mile and Navy Pier area runs 30–50% more for the same quality. Eat one meal a day off the tourist corridor and you’ll noticeably stretch the budget.

Drinks: Chicago bar prices are friendlier than New York or Miami — cocktails run $12–$16 in most neighborhoods, $18–$22 in the tourist-heavy River North bar district. The craft beer scene is strong citywide; a local pint at a neighborhood bar is typically $7–$9.

Tips for Stretching Your $2,000 Chicago Budget

  • Check Midway (MDW) flights first — Southwest often undercuts O’Hare by $40–$80 round-trip
  • A $28 7-day Ventra pass covers all L trains and CTA buses — you won’t need a rental car
  • Stay in the South Loop or northern Loop — 20–30% cheaper than River North with the same L access
  • Book the Architecture Boat Tour early — weekend departures sell out, and it’s the best $47 you’ll spend
  • Lincoln Park Zoo is completely free — spend a half day there before or after the nearby Riverwalk
  • Eat at least one meal a day in Pilsen, Chinatown, or Logan Square — full meals for $12–$18 vs. $30+ on the Mag Mile
